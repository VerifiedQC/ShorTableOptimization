# Recursive PhaseProduct Cost Model

The recursive planner estimates the logical gate count of multiplying two `n`-bit
operands by applying this repository's verified `PhaseProduct` tables
recursively. It is a second, width-dependent measurement alongside the
leaderboard's structural score; `BENCHMARK.md` is authoritative for scoring and
states how the two differ.

The planner lives in `TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/` and runs in Lean. There is
no other implementation.

## Scope

- `PhaseProduct` only. `PhaseTripleProduct` has no gate model and is never
  priced in gates.
- One objective: the minimum ForShor-compatible logical gate count. Recursion
  height and the structural totals are reported for the selected minimum-gate
  plan, not optimized.
- Any positive integer bit length `n`, including widths that were not
  precomputed.
  `n` is an **integer bit length**: `2048` means a 2048-bit integer, not the
  numerical value of an RSA modulus.
- The candidates are `archivedCandidates` in
  `TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/ArchivedCatalog.lean`, generated by
  `scripts/promote.py` from the accepted archive. See "Which tables are
  eligible" below.
- Both `k` and the policy are selected independently at each recursion level.
- ForShor's width and logical-gate definitions are followed except where this
  document identifies a deliberate difference.
- Workspace and ancilla optimization are out of scope.

## Separation of responsibilities

The Lean verifier proves that a policy's symbolic operation program is valid.
That proof is independent of the operand width.

The planner consumes only verified programs and decides how to use them at a
particular width. Champions are chosen by the leaderboard's structural weighted
cost and the planner minimises gate count, so a table that lost its champion slot
can still be the cheapest choice at some width:

```text
verified policies (k and operation program)
                    |
                    v
       recursive planner (n and objective)
                    |
                    v
     selected policy and k at every level
                    |
                    v
        resource estimate and compact plan
```

The submission-facing Lean interface therefore needs no `n` parameter. The
planner owns the width-dependent selection.

## The ForShor companion

The table-operation languages are compatible. Both repositories use `shiftL`,
`shiftR`, `negate`, `addScaled` and `phaseProduct`.

The gate costs are not reproduced here and not copied either. `Reg`, `ExtReg`,
`LowGate`, `LowGateCostModel`, `GateResources`, the Cuccaro resource records and
`shorGateCostModel` itself are the companion's own definitions, imported from the
`Fast_multiplication` dependency. The planner's constants are computed from those
records, so the familiar `9w - 16` and `10w - 14` are theorems
(`cuccaroModAddGateCount_eq`, `cuccaroNegateGateCount_eq`) rather than numbers
transcribed from the companion, and `cuccaroNegateGateCount_eq_model` ties the
planner's constant to what `shorGateCostModel.negate` charges, by `rfl` and with
no axioms.

### What this repository adds on top

Three things the companion does not supply, all under
`TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/`:

- `CompanionTransport.lean` -- the single bridge between this repository's
  `Operations.valid_ops` / `Point` and the companion's. They are distinct
  inductives of identical shape, because the submission path has to stay
  Mathlib-free.
- `CompanionCost.lean` -- a `Nat`-indexed face on cost functions the companion
  states over an `ExtReg` but reads only a width from, so the planner pays no
  `O(w)` register construction per query.
- `CompanionAllocation.lean` and `AllocationAgreement.lean` -- the companion
  bounds its allocation bookkeeping (`<= 2nW`); the planner charges a closed
  form and needs the exact figure, so these evaluate
  `bookkeepingGateCost (compileSignedAllocations ...)` and prove the closed form
  **equal** to it.

The dependency is confined to `TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/**`, enforced by
`scripts/check_mathlib_confinement.py`, which is what keeps the submission axiom
audit reporting `propext` alone.

### The dependency pin, and what moves it

ForShor is a Lake dependency, declared in `lakefile.lean` as the package
`Fast_multiplication` and tracking its `main` branch. Mathlib is required first
so that this repository's pin governs: ForShor's own Mathlib requirement carries
no revision, and Lake resolves a package name once.

Tracking a branch does not make builds float. `lake-manifest.json` records a
resolved commit and is committed, so CI and a fresh clone build that exact
revision. One thing advances it: `.github/workflows/update-forshor.yml`, which
runs `lake update Fast_multiplication` on a daily schedule. It pushes to `main`
without review, which is safe only because it refuses to push unless the
companion's cost model is byte-identical to
`scripts/tests/companion-cost-model.tsv` -- the primitive gate costs, the
allocation costs and the width transitions, which is everything the planner reads
from the companion. If any of that moves, the job fails and leaves `main` alone.

That transcript is the gate on the model, and it is archive-independent by
construction. `scripts/test_cost_model_lean.py` cannot serve as it: that compares
the planner against itself, so a companion change moves both sides together.

To update by hand:

    lake update Fast_multiplication

After any update, check that the planner still reports the same totals, because
these are the figures the write-up quotes:

    lake env lean --run scripts/tests/RecursiveCostOracle.lean \
      --best-known 1024 2048 4096 8192 16384

    1024   1649629   3    147    213
    2048   4343539   4    726    689
    4096   11330660  4    1628   2427
    8192   28618144  4    3016   4888
    16384  71324077  4    4872   7984

(width, gates, recursion height, recursive calls, arithmetic operations; the
command also prints a trailing `k:childWidth:policy` column for the top-level

moved.

### Checking the closed form against the companion's compiler

The oracle checks the planner's *parts*. To check the whole number, the companion
can compile a table to a `LowGate` tree and count it, which is the quantity
`lake exe forshor_emit pp <k> <n> <phi> --flat` prints:

    lake env lean --run scripts/tests/ChampionGateCountDifferential.lean

It prices this repository's promoted champions -- not the companion's standard
table -- both ways, and insists on exact integer agreement; a mismatch prints both
numbers and exits non-zero. It is not in CI: it takes about five minutes, and is
feasible only at `k = 2` and `k = 3`, because the lowered term has
`recursiveCallCount ^ depth` leaves and does not finish at `k >= 4`. The default
sweep is widths 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 at `k = 2` and 20, 24 at `k = 3`, chosen to sit
above each champion's first recursing width.

One subtlety it has to account for, set out in full in
`TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/ChampionLowering.lean`: the companion's
`PhaseLoweringPlan` recurses on exactly one condition, whether the table strictly
contracts the width, with no cost comparison anywhere. The planner also gets to
decline, reporting the schoolbook base case when recursing is dearer. So the
planner answers "what is the cheapest policy at this width" and the compiler
answers "what does this table compile to", and the two coincide only where the
planner's optimum is to recurse. The differential drives the planner's own
per-node charge with the companion's stopping rule, and prints the planner's own
figure beside it so the gap stays visible.

At `k = 2` the two independent computations agree digit for digit:

| k | width | child | closed form | planner min | `LowGate.gateCount` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 8 | 7 | 935 | 320 | 935 |
| 2 | 10 | 8 | 3041 | 500 | 3041 |
| 2 | 12 | 9 | 9395 | 720 | 9395 |
| 2 | 14 | 10 | 9431 | 980 | 9431 |
| 2 | 16 | 11 | 28529 | 1280 | 28529 |

The fourth and sixth columns are the two independent computations. The fifth is
the planner's own rule, and its distance from the other two is the whole content
of the subtlety above: at these widths the champion's optimum is the schoolbook
base case, so the planner declines to recurse where the compiler cannot. That
column is printed, not asserted on.

There are no `k = 3` rows here because the `PhaseProduct` `k = 3` champion
changed at `49eaf8f` and they were never re-measured. Run the differential rather
than quoting a number from here.

## Widths

### Initial widths

The model takes a pair of operand widths `(xWidth, zWidth)`; the balanced profile
maps `n` to `(n, n)`.

For a policy with parameter `k`, ForShor uses a top-heavy split. The lower
`k - 1` limbs have a common width, and the final limb absorbs each operand's
remainder:

```text
limbWidth = min(floor(xWidth / k), floor(zWidth / k))
```

### Width transitions

For each operand, ForShor updates symbolic widths as follows. These are exact,
and `nextSignedWidth` in `TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/Model.lean` is defined
from the companion's own scan:

```text
shiftL(i, amount):
  width[i] = width[i] + amount

shiftR(i, amount):
  width[i] = width[i] - amount, truncated at zero

negate(i):
  width[i] = width[i] + 1

addScaled(dst, src, shift):
  width[dst] = 1 + max(width[dst], width[src] + shift)
```

The child width is one more than the largest width the scan ever requests. These
are safe capacity bounds rather than exact mathematical ranges: an inverse
addition does not make the recorded width shrink. The model retains the bounds so
that its claims stay compatible with ForShor.

`nextBalancedSignedWidth` is an evaluation-oriented array form of the same scan,
used because the companion's `Function.update` chains are too slow to drive a
planner over thousands of widths. Nothing in the type system ties the two
together, so the oracle's `--width-scan` mode checks them against each other.

ForShor pads every recursive child at a level to one common maximum width, and
the planner implements that as its conformance mode. It has no other mode.

## Logical gate costs

One model, `v3` (`forshor-phase-product-gates-v3` in `modelVersion`). It is the
companion's operative `shorGateCostModel = shorGateResourceModel.toCostModel` in
`Framework/Gatecount/ResourceModel.lean`, read from the dependency at the revision
`lake-manifest.json` pins:

- `addScaled`: exact Cuccaro modulo adder,
  `(2w - 6) + (5w - 7) + (2w - 3)` = `9w - 16` for `w >= 3`;
- `negate`: complement plus a constant-one register plus that adder,
  `(w + (2w - 6) + 2) + (5w - 7) + (2w - 3)` = `10w - 14` for `w >= 3`;
- `shiftL` / `shiftR` / `zeroExtend` / `zeroDealloc`: free;
- `signExtend` / `signDealloc`: `n` CNOTs each.

Truncated `Nat` subtraction is deliberate: the companion's definitions are over
`Nat`, so the same truncation applies below the published `w >= 3` regime.

Because `allocChunkGate` sign-extends only the top limb and zero-extends the
other `k - 1` limbs, the exact per-node allocation charge is
`2 * ((W' - topLimb(x)) + (W' - topLimb(z)))`, not `4kW'`. The `4kW'` term is a
bound proved in the companion's `GateCount/PhaseProduct/Lemmas.lean`
(`lgc_allocs_le`, `lgc_deallocs_le`) for the asymptotic recurrence, and is not a
cost the operative model charges.

These values are exact with respect to the declared abstract cost model. They are
not hardware gate counts and do not include routing or error correction.

### What is integrated, and what is not

The *circuit-level* model is integrated. ForShor's own recurrence-level costing,
`phaseArithmeticOpCost` in its `Implementation/GateCount/Definitions.lean`, still
charges the conservative `rippleAdderGateBound = 9w + 2`. The model here is
ForShor's recurrence shape with ForShor's circuit constants -- a combination
ForShor does not itself define, and it should not be described as a number
ForShor computes.

The oracle refuses a `--model=<name>` flag by name rather than ignoring it, so an
invocation naming a retired model fails with that reason instead of being read as
a width.

## Recursive optimization

For a verified policy `P`, let `children(P, widths)` be the recursive
`PhaseProduct` calls found by interpreting its operation program at the supplied
widths. The minimum logical-gate cost is:

```text
G(widths) = min(
  baseGateCost(widths),
  min over eligible (k, P) of
    arithmeticGateCost(P, widths)
      + sum(G(childWidths) for childWidths in children(P, widths))
)
```

The base case is always an available candidate. A recursive candidate is eligible
only when every child is strictly smaller than its parent. That ensures
termination and lets the model choose the base case on cost rather than at an
arbitrary fixed threshold.

Widths are not precomputed. The planner has a dense all-width table
(`buildPlanTable`) as a readable reference and a sparse traversal of reachable
widths (`buildSparsePlans`) as the form it evaluates; the oracle's
`--dense-sparse` mode checks that the two select the same plans. Runtime depends
mainly on the number of distinct widths reached, not on the numerical size of
`n`. The machine-readable result has one aggregated entry per recursion level
rather than expanding identical child nodes, which is sound because conformance
mode pads every child at a level to one common width.

### Which tables are eligible

`scripts/promote.py` writes `archivedCandidates` into
`TableGeneration/RecursiveCost/ArchivedCatalog.lean`: every accepted
`PhaseProduct` table, minus those excluded by a **layer bound**. A table deeper
than the current per-target champion at the same arity is dropped, because the
gate model charges nothing for layers and without the bound a table could buy
gates with depth for free.

A dropped table stays archived on `submission-results`. It is simply not planned
over, so it cannot move the published number. This is the only catalogue; there
is no unbounded variant and no flag that selects one.

## Reported metrics

For the selected minimum-gate plan the planner reports:

- the logical gate count: arithmetic gates at a node plus every recursive child;
- the recursion height, `H(base) = 0` and `H(node) = 1 + max(H(child))`, which is
  the number of decomposition levels on the longest branch and does not include
  arithmetic within a level;
- the total recursive `PhaseProduct` call count;
- the total symbolic arithmetic operation count;
- the selected policy, `k` and child width.

Full circuit depth, workspace, ancilla and balanced objectives can be added later
without changing the verified table-policy interface.

## Validation

`scripts/test_cost_model_lean.py` is what CI runs, on the ForShor dependency
bump. It compares Lean against Lean, and no theorem covers what it
checks:

- `--width-scan`: the array scan against the companion's `Function.update` scan;
- `--dense-sparse`: the dense and sparse planners agree, over a width list that
  straddles the 32/64/128 boundaries;
- `--reference`: the two reference candidates agree with themselves;
- `--catalog`: the catalogue is ordered by arity and covers `k = 2` through `16`;
- default mode: two golden plan lines, so a silent change in the plan format or
  in the costs is caught.

Alongside it:

- `scripts/tests/companion-cost-model.tsv`, the pinned transcript described
  above, which is the gate on a companion cost-model change;
- `canonicalAllocationCost_eq`, which proves the closed-form allocation charge
  equal to the companion's compiled figure, so no test is needed for it;
- `scripts/tests/ChampionGateCountDifferential.lean`, run by hand, which prices
  promoted champions against the companion's compiler.

The model version and objective are published with results so that a future
ForShor cost-model change does not silently reinterpret an old estimate.
